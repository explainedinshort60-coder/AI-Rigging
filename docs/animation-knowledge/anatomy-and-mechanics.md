# Anatomy and body mechanics for cutout animators

Working rules for posing and keying 2D rigs, and for reviewing them. Labels: **[M]** measured data, **[C]** canonical teaching (books, studio practice), **[V]** convention or proposed starting point: tune by eye. Sources at the end.

## 1. Proportions (heads tall)

| Figure | Heads | Landmarks below the chin |
|---|---|---|
| Average adult (Richer) [C] | 7.5 | nipples 1, navel 2, crotch 3; body midpoint at the pubic bone |
| Idealized (Loomis) [C] | 8 (heroic 8.5 to 9) | same 1 / 2 / 3; male shoulders 2⅓ heads wide |
| Child age 12 / 8 / 1 [C] | 7 / 6.25 / 4.5 | midpoint rises toward the navel as the child gets younger |
| Preschool cartoon hero [V] | 3 to 4 | short torso, legs about 1 to 1.5 heads |
| Chibi [V] | 2 to 2.5 | body about 1 head, no visible neck, mitten hands |

- Widths [C]: male shoulders 2 heads (Richer), female shoulders about 1⅔, hips about 1.5. Foot slightly over 1 head long.
- Limbs [C]: shoulder-to-elbow equals elbow-to-knuckles. Femur about 2 heads; shin plus foot about 2. Middle finger about as long as the rest of the hand.
- Conventions [V]: elbows at the waist, wrists at the crotch, fingertips mid-thigh, hand about face length.
- **Rule:** put the character's proportions in heads on the model sheet and check every key pose against it. A limb that changes length between keys (in head units) is "rubber limb" drift unless squash and stretch is a style choice and volume is kept.
- Big-head characters [V]: the centre of gravity rises toward the chest, so head tilts and leans move the balance much more. Squash and stretch on the torso does more of the acting than elbows and knees.

## 2. Where the pivots really are

| Part | True pivot | Classic cutout mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Upper arm | Ball of the humerus, just under the shoulder tip [C] | A fixed socket with no shoulder above it |
| Shoulder girdle | Collarbone pivots at the pit of the neck (sternal notch) [C] | Missing entirely |
| Thigh | Femoral head at about crotch height, lower and more central than the belt [C] | Pivot at the belt line: legs swing from the waist and grow in kicks |
| Knee | Femur-tibia joint at the bottom of the kneecap, not its centre [C] | Pivot at the kneecap centre |
| Head | Top of the spine, behind and below the ear, behind the jaw angle [V] | Pivot at the chin: nods swing the face like a hinge |
| Wrist / ankle | Wrist crease; ankle bones, slightly behind the arch [V] | Mid-palm; mid-foot |
| Torso | Two rigid masses (ribcage, pelvis) joined by the lumbar spine; the bend pivot is at waist level [C] | One stiff torso layer |

**The shoulder rule [M].** Raising the arm to 180° is about 120° at the shoulder joint plus about 60° of shoulder-blade rotation: a 2:1 rhythm. From 30° to 90° the blade adds about 20°. Past 90° the collarbone rotates back 30 to 50° and **elevates up to 15°**. So any arm raised past about 60 to 90° needs a shoulder layer that rises (8 to 15° in 2D) and moves in toward the ear. A rig without one: request it, or cheat with a raised-shoulder drawing swap.

**Spine shape [C].** The neck curves forward, the chest back, the lower back forward. In side view the neck leaves the ribcage at a forward angle; it never stacks straight up.

## 3. Joint limits (degrees, from the rest drawing)

Clinical ranges [M] (sources disagree by 10 to 30° on some joints; use the higher number as the hard limit and the lower as the comfortable acting range):

| Joint | Range | Rig rule |
|---|---|---|
| Shoulder | 180 forward or up, 50 to 60 back, 180 out | Past 90°: shoulder layer rises |
| Elbow | 0 to 140-145, never below 0 | Rest at 10 to 20° bent; never lock at 0 except a deliberate push |
| Forearm twist | 80 / 80 | In 2D this is a hand drawing swap, not rotation |
| Wrist | 60-80 flex, 60-70 extend, 20 thumb side, 30 pinky side | Keep within ±15° of the forearm when relaxed; beyond the limits reads as a broken wrist |
| Neck | 60 forward, 75 back, 45 tilt, 80 turn | Turns past about 30° are a head-angle drawing swap, not a rotation |
| Chest on pelvis | 45-50 forward, 25 back, 25 side, 30 twist | Bigger bends need the hips to tilt too |
| Hip (thigh) | 100-120 forward, 30 back, 40-45 out | Big kicks tilt the pelvis |
| Knee | 0 to 135-150, never below 0 | Rest at about 5° bent |
| Ankle | about 20 up, 40-50 down | Toe layer pivots at the ball of the foot |

**2D caveat.** In a front view a rotated shoulder (palm out) flips which way the elbow bends on screen, so a big reversed elbow bend on an arm can be correct. What reads as broken is a limb that sits **just past straight** (8 to 35°) or **snaps through straight** in 1 to 3 frames. Knees in side view have no such excuse.

## 4. Balance and weight

- **Centre of gravity [M]:** about 55% of height, just in front of the sacrum (a little below the navel, inside the pelvis). A whole leg's centre of mass is just above the knee, so lifting a leg moves the balance noticeably.
- **Plumb line check [V]:** drop a vertical from the pit of the neck. One-leg stance: it lands on or near the inner ankle of the standing foot. Two legs: between the feet, nearer the foot carrying more weight. Outside the feet means falling or stepping, which is only right if that is the action.
- **Contrapposto [C]:** weight on one leg. That side's hip goes **up**, the shoulder on that side goes **down** (hips and shoulders tilt opposite ways), and the torso leans so the head stays over the support foot. Hips and shoulders tilting the same way is a flag.
- **Counterbalance [V]:** when an arm, leg or carried object reaches out, the torso, pelvis or other arm shifts the opposite way.
- **Hips lead [C]:** in every weight shift the pelvis moves first, the spine follows, the head settles last. Contacts (feet, hands, seat) never slide while they carry load.

## 5. Movement, with frame counts at 24 fps

**Walks [C, Williams via Monmouth; M for cadence].** Frames per step: 4 very fast run · 6 run or very fast walk · 8 slow run or cartoon walk · **12 brisk natural walk (120 steps/min)** · 14 average adult (measured 100 to 120 steps/min) · 16 stroll · 20 old or tired · 24 slow step.
- 24-frame cycle (12 per step): contacts on 1 and 13, downs on 4 and 16, push-off and up on 10 and 22, passing between. Render 1 to 24 for a clean loop (frame 25 = frame 1).
- The body is lowest on the down and highest at passing/up. Arms swing opposite to the legs. Hips twist so the legs do not flap as a separate unit.
- Foot roll [V]: heel strike with the toe up, foot flat on the down, heel peels at push-off, toe pivots last.

**Runs [C].** At least 1 to 2 frames with both feet off the ground, a deeper down, more arm swing. Lean with hips, torso and head together, hips slightly leading. Errors: overreach (braking), landing behind (slipping), toe drag (tired), stomping, washing-machine torso.

**Sit / stand [C].** 18 to 28 frames each: anticipation 6 to 10, action 8 to 12, settle 4 to 6, plus 1 to 3 frames of compression on seat contact.

**Standing weight shift [V].** By analogy, about 18 to 28 frames: a small push away from the target foot (6 to 10), hips travel (8 to 12), spine then head settle (4 to 6).

**Lift and push [C].** The hips and legs commit before the object moves. A heavy object lags a few frames behind the hands. Arms-only effort, a weightless box that rises the moment it is touched, and sliding hands or feet are the classic mistakes.

**Jumps and falls [M physics].** Free-fall spacing grows 1 : 3 : 5 : 7 per frame (not doubling).
- Drop of 0.5 m: about 8 frames. 1 m: about 11. 2 m: about 15.
- Jump rising 0.25 / 0.5 / 1 m: about 11 / 15 / 22 frames of airtime, equal up and down.
- A 1 m drop that takes 24 frames reads floaty. Cartoon timing usually goes shorter, not longer.

**Bounces [M physics].** Each bounce keeps e² of its height and e of its airtime. Lively ball e ≈ 0.8 (64% height); dead or heavy e ≈ 0.3 to 0.5, settling in 1 or 2 bounces.

**Heavy vs light [C].** Heavy: longer anticipation, slow out of rest, little squash, short sharp impact, visible recoil in the character. Light: quick starts, more squash and stretch, more bounces, more drag.

**Successive breaking of joints [C].** Force travels shoulder, elbow, wrist, fingers (hips and spine lead the whole body). On a point, the elbow dips first, the wrist leads, the fingers arrive last. Offset each joint 1 to 2 frames [V]; 2 to 4 for loose parts (hair, tails, ears, scarves).

**Head turns [V].** The eyes lead the head by 2 to 4 frames. The head dips on a slight downward arc through the middle of the turn. Blink mid-turn to hide the head-angle swap. Ease out, fast through the middle, overshoot and settle.

## 6. Faces (FACS)

Action units [M, coding system]: AU1 inner brow up · AU2 outer brow up · AU4 brows down and together · AU5 upper lid up · AU6 cheeks up · AU7 lids tight · AU9 nose wrinkle · AU12 lip corners up · AU15 lip corners down · AU20 lips stretched sideways · AU23 lips tight · AU26 jaw drop.

| Emotion | Recipe | What a rig needs |
|---|---|---|
| Real smile (Duchenne) | 6 + 12 | Mouth swap **plus** cheeks push the lower lids up and the eyes narrow. A mouth-only smile reads fake |
| Sadness | 1 + 4 + 15 | Inner brow corners UP while the brows pull together (slanted worried brow), mouth corners down |
| Surprise | 1 + 2 + 5 + 26 | Whole brow up, lids wide, jaw dropped with relaxed lips |
| Fear | 1 + 2 + 4 + 5 + 7 + 20 + 26 | Like surprise but with lips stretched sideways and brows pulled together |
| Anger | 4 + 5 + 7 + 23 | Brows down and in, lids tense, lips pressed |
| Disgust | 9 + 15 + 17 | Nose wrinkle, corners down, chin up |
| Contempt | one-sided 12 + 14 | Defined by asymmetry |

Add a small left-right difference to every held expression [V] (one brow higher, one corner more pulled) except deliberately neutral ones.

## 7. Anatomy review checklist (every key pose)

1. Hinge just past straight the wrong way, or snapping through straight (elbows, knees).
2. Limbs locked at exactly 0°: keep 5 to 15° of bend unless it is a push or a standing leg.
3. Arm above 90° with no shoulder rise.
4. Wrist beyond about 60-80° flex, 60-70° extend, 20/30° sideways.
5. Floating head: no neck, or the head pivoting at the chin.
6. Rubber limbs: segment lengths change between keys in head units.
7. Sliding feet or hands on load-bearing contacts.
8. Twins: left and right mirror each other in pose or timing.
9. Off balance: the plumb line from the neck pit lands outside the feet when it is not a fall or a step.
10. Hips and shoulders tilting the same way in a weight-shift pose.
11. Thigh pivoting at the belt line.
12. A smile without the cheeks and lower lids moving.

`tools/anim-lint.js` checks items 1, 3, 8 and parts of 2 and 7 automatically on AI Rigging projects.
