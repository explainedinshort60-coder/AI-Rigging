# Good and bad shapes and poses

A frame-by-frame rulebook for animators and art directors. Each rule: the bad version, the good version, why. **[P]** = principle (a readability problem pros agree on). **[T]** = taste or convention (break it on purpose, for a reason). Sources at the end.

## 1. Shape design

| # | Bad | Good | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Head, torso, legs (or eyes, nose, mouth) all about the same size | A clear big / medium / small hierarchy | Equal sizes are monotonous; "make big things bigger and small things smaller" [P] |
| S2 | Circles, squares and triangles mixed with none in charge | One dominant shape that fits the personality, others as accents | The shape-meaning convention only reads when one shape leads [T] |
| S3 | Many small, equally weighted details spread evenly | Simple masses first, details clustered in one place | Readability (KISS) [P] |

Shape meaning [T, convention]: circle = soft, friendly, safe · square = stable, stubborn, dependable · triangle = energy, danger, direction.

## 2. Line, rhythm and the line of action

| # | Bad | Good | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Vertical spine, a pose built from stacked parts | One clear C or S curve through head, spine and weight leg (the line of action) | Energy and a clear read (Blair) [P] |
| L2 | Both contours of a limb bow out the same way (sausage) or both straight (tube) | **Straight on the stretched side, curve on the squashed side** | "A straight line is the symbol for a stretch, and a bent or folded line is the symbol for a squash" (Stanchfield) [P] |
| L3 | Shoulders, hips and head all level | Opposing tilts: shoulders against hips, head tilted | Tilts force an attitude choice [P] |
| L4 | A limb kinks against the line of action for no reason | Curves hand off into each other; the line of action carries into arms and legs | Flow [P] |
| L5 | Every key the same curve | A C reads as one big force (push, pull, lean); an S reads relaxed or graceful. Reverse the curve between keys (C to reverse C) for snap | [T, craft convention] |

## 3. Tension and pinch

| # | Bad | Good | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | The torso bends but both sides keep the same length | Inside of the bend short, curved, creased; outside long and straighter | Squash and stretch inside a pose [P] |
| T2 | Two parallel, evenly bulging contours from shoulder to wrist | Offset bulges (bicep high on one side, forearm mass lower on the other), straight against curve | [P] |
| T3 | Hair or cape stretched on the side where the body squashes | Secondary elements squash and stretch on the same sides as the body | Otherwise the ideas cancel out (Stanchfield) [P] |

## 4. Tangents, parallels, twins

| # | Bad | Good | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Two outlines that just touch: hand edge on the hip, head top on the frame edge, shoulder line running into a background line | Overlap clearly or separate clearly | Tangents flatten depth, catch the eye, and blur which limb belongs to whom [P]; flat graphic styles may use them [T] |
| P2 | Both arms doing the same thing, mirrored; feet planted the same | Different angles and heights per side; one arm acting, one resting | Twinning is "usually the death-knell" of life [P]; fine for model sheets, robots, prayer hands [T] |
| P3 | Arm parallel to the leg or to the torso edge on the same side | Tilt one so they converge or diverge | Parallels stiffen a pose [P] |
| P4 | Fingers fanned at equal angles; spikes or buttons evenly spaced and sized | Group them (fingers 2+2 or 3+1), vary spacing and size | Even repeats read mechanical [P] |
| P5 | A pose dominated by plumb verticals and level horizontals | Diagonals from the spine, a tilted head, props, hair | [P] |
| P6 | Character square to camera, head and torso both frontal | 3/4 view, or head and torso turned differently | Depth and fewer twins [P]; front is valid for direct address [T] |

Twinning also happens in **time**: limbs hitting keys on the same frame. Offset 1 to 2 frames.

## 5. Silhouette and negative space

Test: fill the character black. If the action and attitude still read, the pose passes. Also test small (downscaled), and with no context ("what is this character doing?").

| # | Bad | Good |
|---|---|---|
| N1 | Acting hand in front of the chest: in black it vanishes | Hand or forearm breaks the body outline, with a gap of negative space |
| N2 | A limb fully hidden behind another | At least the elbow or knee and part of both segments show |
| N3 | Two elbows or hands stacked on the same spot | Offset them on screen |
| N4 | Two equal gaps under both arms | One big gap and one small one, or one closed |
| N5 | A prop (sword, phone) held flat against the body | Held out into clear space |
| N6 | An action pointed straight at the lens (the arm becomes a dot) | Angle it 20 to 45° off the lens axis, or enlarge the near hand as a 2D foreshortening cheat |

## 6. Faces

| # | Bad | Good | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Brows, eye line and mouth all horizontal and parallel | Features converge or open in a cone toward the look direction | Singh (Disney) [P] |
| F2 | Both mouth corners showing on a turned head (flat sticker mouth) | Far corner tucked behind the cheek; lips wrap the teeth | Singh [P] |
| F3 | Cheek contour just touching the eye or jaw line | Cheek clearly overlaps the far eye or breaks the jaw outline in 3/4 | Tangent rule [P] |
| F4 | One eye shows far more white than the other | Matching white-to-iris ratio; the upper-lid peak sits over the pupil | Singh [P] |
| F5 | Head facing straight into camera | Head angled, with a head-neck line of action | Singh [P] |
| F6 | A smile that only moves the mouth | Cheeks lift and the lower lids push up (Duchenne) | FACS [M] |

Wide-set eyes read younger or cuter, close-set more intense: convention [T].

## 7. Staging for camera

| # | Bad | Good |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Flat front view | 3/4 with the head turned differently from the torso |
| C2 | Action straight into the lens | 20 to 45° off axis or a size cheat |
| C3 | Frame edge cutting at a wrist, knee or ankle | Crop mid-limb |
| C4 | Both hands equally busy, or the acting hand lost against the torso | One hand is the focal point, in negative space, near the face or the object of attention |

Flow lines in the pose and the strongest contrast should lead to one focal point at a time.

## 8. Pose check (run on every key)

1. Line of action: one C or S, not stiff, not wobbly.
2. Straight against curve on bent limbs and torso; pinch on the inside of every bend.
3. Shoulders, hips and head tilted, not level and parallel.
4. No twins in pose or timing.
5. No tangents with other parts, props or the frame edge.
6. Silhouette reads in black; the acting hand is outside the body.
7. Negative space: varied gap sizes.
8. Big / medium / small, details clustered.
9. 3/4 over flat front unless the shot wants direct address.
10. Face: features in a cone, far mouth corner tucked, matching eye whites, smile with cheeks.

## Measurable proxies (for automated review, thresholds are proposals to calibrate)

- Line of action: curvature sign changes along the spine (0 with near-zero curvature = stiff, 1 = C, 2 = S, more = wobbly).
- Twins: mirror the silhouette and measure overlap (IoU > about 0.9 in an acting pose = flag); left/right joint angles within about 10°.
- Parallels: non-connected limb segments within about 5 to 8° of each other.
- Tangents: separate contours within about 2 px over a short run, with no overlap.
- Silhouette: solidity (area / convex hull area) and the size spread of negative-space holes; the acting hand inside the torso polygon = flag.
- Level lines: shoulder, hip and eye-line angles all within about 3° of horizontal.
- Even repeats: low variation in the gaps between fingers, spikes, buttons.
- Focal point: the contrast or saliency peak should land on the face or acting hand.
